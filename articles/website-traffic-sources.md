# Website traffic sources

Originally published at <https://vaneform.com/guides/website-traffic-sources> on 2026-10-04 by Kai · 中文版: <https://vaneform.com/zh/guides/website-traffic-sources>

## Summary

A website's traffic sources show where the next investigation should begin. This guide explains what channel labels such as Direct, Organic Search, Referrals and Email describe, and how owner analytics and outside estimates classify activity differently.

Through constructed examples it shows how a channel's share can fall while its volume barely moves, and why you must know a percentage's denominator: a share of all visits, a share within search, or a share within referrals. It then turns each channel into a concrete next check. For search, look at receiving pages and branded vs problem queries. For referrals, look at the linking page and its destination. For direct, look at repeat use and missing source information. The output should be one small decision: a page or campaign to inspect, the reason, and the result you need. It also warns that categories may not be mutually exclusive and that small rounding differences can remain when a full channel mix is displayed, so totals should be checked before they are quoted.

## Key points and data

- Illustrative site: 100,000 → 120,000 visits, with search supplying 12,000 of the net 20,000 gain.
- Direct's share can fall from 50% to 40% while its volume falls only 4%.
- 20% referral share of 100,000 visits is about 20,000 visits; a sub-breakdown share must not be applied to the total.
- Google Analytics defines Direct as arrivals via a saved link or typed URL (checked 2026-10-16).
- End every channel review with one page or campaign to inspect.
