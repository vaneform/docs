# How to find low competition keywords that fit your page

Originally published at <https://vaneform.com/guides/find-low-competition-keywords> on 2026-10-04 by Kai · 中文版: <https://vaneform.com/zh/guides/find-low-competition-keywords>

## Summary

A low keyword-difficulty score earns a closer look, not a commitment. This guide combines difficulty, search intent and real results-page evidence to find lower-competition opportunities that your page can actually win, using a CSV-cleaning product for operations teams as the teaching case.

It recommends narrowing from a real constraint (for example, de-duplicating on an order ID plus a line-item ID) rather than adding arbitrary adjectives to a head term, then opening the leading results and trying the task yourself. A visible weakness should be specific enough to fix. If a query is dominated by working upload tools, that is a product requirement, not a content gap. The guide covers writing an opportunity note with a concrete contribution, when to stop researching, and reasons to reject an apparently easy keyword, such as an output you cannot maintain or a weak-looking result that has a trusted community behind it. After publishing, check whether the page attracts the intended queries and whether visitors can finish the task.

## Key points and data

- Ahrefs ties Keyword Difficulty to the top ten organic results and recommends manual review.
- Narrow by a real constraint, not by word count.
- Results dominated by working tools mean the page must be a tool.
- Keep query, checked results, market and date with each opportunity note.
- A modest score can still be a poor assignment for your site.
