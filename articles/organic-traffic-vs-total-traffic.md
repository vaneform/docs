# Organic traffic vs total traffic: understand ETV

Originally published at <https://vaneform.com/guides/organic-traffic-vs-total-traffic> on 2026-10-08 by Kai · 中文版: <https://vaneform.com/zh/guides/organic-traffic-vs-total-traffic>

## Summary

A website report may show 100,000 visits while a keyword tool shows 12,000 organic "ETV". Neither is wrong. They measure different things. This guide separates four measures that often get mixed together: whole-site visits, the organic channel estimate within those visits, keyword-based estimated traffic volume (ETV, modelled from rankings, search volume and click-through rates) and Search Console clicks.

It explains why an ETV figure can move while tracked rankings look stable (sample coverage and demand change too), and why dividing a US-market ETV by a global visit total produces a meaningless "search share". It also shows how share and volume can move in opposite directions. The guide ends with a diagnostic order for when ETV and your own clicks disagree: scope, coverage, demand, click-through rate, and then the competitor's ranking pages, plus a short measurement note template for colleagues. Throughout, the advice is to choose the smallest conclusion that still supports the decision in front of you, and to save the market, observation date and keyword rows with every comparison.

## Key points and data

- Teaching example: 1,000 monthly searches at an assumed 8% CTR contributes 80 estimated visits.
- Keep whole-site visits, organic channel estimate, keyword ETV and Search Console clicks in separate columns.
- A global 100,000-visit estimate and a US 12,000 ETV cannot be divided into a search share.
- Organic share can rise 10 percentage points while implied organic visits fall 20%.
- Diagnose ETV/click gaps by scope, then coverage, demand and CTR.
