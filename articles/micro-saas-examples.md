# Micro SaaS examples: lessons from focused real products

Originally published at <https://vaneform.com/guides/micro-saas-examples> on 2026-10-04 by Kai · 中文版: <https://vaneform.com/zh/guides/micro-saas-examples>

## Summary

Lists of "profitable Micro SaaS ideas" rarely explain the work behind a product. This guide studies two focused, real products through their public workflows instead: ScreenshotOne, which turns a browser screenshot into an API call, and Buttondown, which helps people publish a recurring newsletter. Observations were checked on 9 October 2026.

For each product it reads the public documentation to understand what the user actually brings and receives, then turns that into a testable experiment: who the user is, what result they can verify, how success is measured, and who pays and who repairs things when a delivery fails. It also shows how keyword intent differs around the same job ("screenshot API" vs "full page screenshot") and why keywords come after choosing the job. The takeaway is a short, testable brief rather than an idea list, and the evidence it produces may support software, a small service, a narrower feature or a decision to stop.

## Key points and data

- ScreenshotOne estimated visits: 106,042 (June), 108,135 (July), 120,401 (August 2026), medium confidence.
- Buttondown had no stored Vaneform public report on the observation date, which the guide records as a coverage gap.
- Measure a screenshot experiment by requested pages, usable outputs, manual fixes and total delivery time.
- Identify both the person who uses a weekly output and the person who pays for it.
- Choose the job first, then the keywords.
