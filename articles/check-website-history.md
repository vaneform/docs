# How to check website history with dated evidence

Originally published at <https://vaneform.com/guides/check-website-history> on 2026-10-08 by Kai · 中文版: <https://vaneform.com/zh/guides/check-website-history>

## Summary

A domain registered years ago can host a product launched last month. This guide shows how to establish what a website used to be, and when its current offer appeared, using archived captures, registration records and a dated timeline that keeps uncertainty visible.

A fictional worked example brackets a change between two captures without inventing a launch day, keeping "registration event", "tool visible by" and "launch date: unconfirmed" as separate fields. The guide explains how to read Wayback Machine timestamps (yyyymmddhhmmss), how RDAP replaced WHOIS for registration data, and why archive gaps (pages not discovered, inaccessible or excluded) are themselves part of the evidence. Vaneform's Website history view links available captures and exports them as CSV. It ends with a stopping rule: stop when you can state the current purpose, the earliest evidence for it and the uncertainty that still matters. If a traffic increase follows a visible site change, the sequence stays a hypothesis to investigate, not a proven cause.

## Key points and data

- Domain age, first capture and product age are three different facts with different evidence.
- Wayback replay URLs encode the capture time as yyyymmddhhmmss.
- ICANN describes RDAP as the replacement for the WHOIS protocol (checked 2026-10-16).
- Keep one timeline row per inspected capture and separate visible claims from confirmed facts.
- Vaneform's Website history view exports capture dates and URLs as CSV.
